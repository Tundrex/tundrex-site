The blind spot

Why nobody talks about gut readiness

It isn't that gut health doesn't matter in a crisis. It's that it slips through the cracks of how prep plans get built.

REASON 01

Prep lists are written for logistics, not people.

The official guidance covers quantities: three days of supplies for evacuation and two weeks at home.[2] It tells you what to store. It says little about the person who has to live on those supplies for weeks.

REASON 02

Most probiotics were never built for a kit.

Many probiotics are sold in the refrigerated aisle or in one bottle you open over and over. If a supplement depends on a working fridge, it doesn't belong in a grid-down plan, so most preppers leave the whole category out.

REASON 03

The menu changes quietly, then all at once.

Less fresh produce, less fiber, more salt, different water, broken sleep and stress. Federal health guidance lists diet changes, travel, low fiber and dehydration as common causes of constipation.[4] It isn't dramatic, but over two weeks it's the kind of thing that wears down morale.

REASON 04

It's invisible until it isn't.

You can see a low water barrel and count cans on a shelf. You can't see your gut. Because nothing reminds you to plan for it, the routine you rely on every day is the first one dropped when plans change.

Prepping the pantry is half the job. The other half is the person eating from it.
